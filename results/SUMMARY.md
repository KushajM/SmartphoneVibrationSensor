## Model names used throughout

| name | what it is |
|---|---|
| T1_textbook | Threshold rule a first attempt would use: z-axis RMS decides off/on, the biggest FFT peak decides the speed. No ML. |
| T2_physics | Threshold rule on noise-hardened physics features: harmonic-comb SNR (off/on, constant-false-alarm threshold) + comb fundamental f0 (speed). No ML. |
| kNN / LR _clean | k-nearest-neighbours / logistic regression trained only on the 80 real recordings; features picked by nested selection. |
| kNN / LR _aug | Same models trained on the real recordings PLUS noise-augmented copies of them (calibrated noise), so they learn to ignore noise. |
| HLR_clean / HLR_aug | Two-stage logistic regression: stage A decides 'fan on?', stage B decides 'which speed?' (only for on). **HLR_aug is the final model.** |
| HLR_aug_known | Final model evaluated when the tested noise type WAS in its training augmentation (normal deployment). All other *_aug results use leave-one-noise-group-out: the tested noise type was never seen in training. |

## 2. Preprocessing choices

- **Resampling 400.108 -> 400 Hz with a Kaiser-windowed sinc** (32 taps, beta 8). Linear interpolation was rejected: because the two grids slide past each other, its gain at ~100 Hz varies with the fractional offset (|1-mu+mu e^-jw|), shrinking the dominant line to 0.69 of its true size and averaging 0.75 inside the analysis window - this was the 'start-locked decay' seen earlier. `figures/p1_resampling.png` (theory curve overlays the measurement).
- Gravity removal: 4th-order Butterworth high-pass at 2.0 Hz, forward-backward (zero phase), applied to the full 7 s record before trimming to the central 5 s. `figures/p1_gravity_removal.png`.
- Hampel despiker (+-5 samples, 6 sigma) before resampling; it flags 0 samples in the real data.
- Spectra: mean Welch (Hann, 1024 pts, 0.39 Hz) for plots; median Welch (512 pts, 75 % overlap) summed over the three axes (orientation-invariant) for the robust features.

## 7. Limits - what these results do not show

- One fan, one phone, one mount, one evening. Day-to-day supply-voltage changes (+-6 % statutory) move the fan speed by +-2.5-4.4 %, but the speed classes are only ~7 % apart and every classifier fails beyond ~3 %: this is a change of the measured quantity, not noise. Re-calibrating thresholds with one recording per speed at deployment, or measuring the mains voltage, is the fix.
- A foreign machine whose tone lands on a fan line (e.g. a 2-pole compressor at ~2x48 Hz on speed1's 96 Hz line) is physically indistinguishable from the fan when the other harmonics are buried - its amplitude is uncalibrated.
- Much worse sensors (>= ~300 ug/sqrt(Hz), 12-bit ADCs) or apps capped at 200 Hz lose the dominant line.
- Noise models are calibrated, not measured in situ: footstep/knock amplitudes come from literature anchors, re-taping effects (orientation/gain) are assumptions; all are flagged in section 3.
